# Source quality

Research quality is mostly source quality. Prefer primary, downgrade the rest.

## Evidence hierarchy (best → weakest)

1. Primary data / official records: papers, standards, filings, official docs, source code, datasets.
2. First-party statements: maintainer blogs, release notes, company announcements.
3. Reputable secondary: established press, textbooks, peer-reviewed reviews.
4. Community: forums, Q&A, social posts — use only for leads, never as the sole basis for a factual claim.
5. Aggregators / SEO content farms / AI-generated listicles — avoid as evidence.

## SIFT (apply to every non-primary source)

- **Stop** — don't accept it because it ranks or reads well.
- **Investigate** — who wrote it, when, and why? Conflicts of interest?
- **Find** — corroborate with an independent source.
- **Trace** — follow the claim back to the primary source and cite that.

## Red flags

Unsourced claims; a single study stated as settled fact; undated or stale
material where freshness matters; cherry-picked statistics; undisclosed
conflicts of interest; overgeneralization; content that exists only to rank.

## Rules

- Convergence is not proof: three low-quality sources agreeing is shared noise.
- Weight practitioners and primary authors over commentators.
- Use *via negativa*: name the sources you exclude and why, in one line.
- If only primary sources exist and they conflict, present both with their methods.
